# Mass sentiment

Price is set by people. When almost everyone feels the same way about the market at the same time,
that agreement itself becomes information — usually a warning. Mass sentiment tools try to measure
that mood. The best known in crypto is the Fear & Greed index. Like every tool in this course, it is
useful only once you know what it measures and what it doesn't.

## What the Fear & Greed index actually is

**What it is.** The index (the widely used one is published by alternative.me) is a single number
from 0 to 100: 0 is "extreme fear", 100 is "extreme greed". It is a *composite* — it does not
measure one clean thing, it blends several inputs and boils them down to one score. The published
version weights them roughly like this:

- **Volatility (~25%)** — how turbulent price is versus its own recent average. Calm, orderly moves
  read as confidence (greedy); sudden, violent swings read as panic (fearful). *Why it is in there:*
  fear shows up as jagged, whippy price before people can put it into words. Computed entirely from
  the price series.
- **Market momentum and volume (~25%)** — how strong and stretched recent buying is versus normal.
  Heavy, sustained buying into a rising market is the fingerprint of a crowd piling in. *Why:* greed
  *is* chasing, and chasing is visible as momentum and volume. Also computed straight from price and
  volume.
- **Social media (~15%)** — the volume and tone of posts about crypto. *Why:* a flood of excited
  posting is the signature of a crowd that is emotionally, not analytically, engaged.
- **Bitcoin dominance** — whether money is crowding into Bitcoin or spilling out into speculative
  alts. *Why:* in a scare, money flees to the "safe" corner of crypto (dominance rises → fear). When
  the crowd feels bold it chases riskier alts (dominance falls → greed).
- **Search trends** — Google search interest in crypto terms. *Why:* a spike in "bitcoin crash" or
  "is bitcoin dead" is fear; a surge in "buy bitcoin" is greed.

**Why this matters — the half that is really just price.** Volatility and momentum are both derived
from price itself, and together they are about half the score. So the index is, to a large degree, a
re-description of recent price action. It is *not* an independent second opinion that confirms what
price is doing.

**In practice.** Imagine BTC grinds smoothly from 40,000 to 60,000 over three weeks, with only
shallow dips along the way. Watch the needles. Volatility is low and orderly (greedy) and momentum
and volume are strong and stretched (greedy). Timelines fill with celebration and price targets
(greedy), and "buy bitcoin" searches spike (greedy). The composite prints something like 88 —
extreme greed. But the rising price *fed most of those inputs*. The index did not independently
discover euphoria; the price did the work, and the index simply put a name on it. That is why a
fresh price high and an extreme-greed reading almost always arrive together.

## What it measures — and what it doesn't

It measures the tone of the recent past: a fast rally with heavy volume and euphoric posts scores
greedy; a sharp drop with panic search spikes scores fearful. That is a real, useful summary of the
crowd's current mood.

It does not measure any of the things a beginner assumes it does. It does not know the future. It
does not tell you where price is going next, when a move will turn, or how much room is left. Its
heaviest inputs are computed from price that has *already* printed. So the index can only ever tell
you how the recent past felt: it is a lagging, backward-looking read, not a forecast.

:::note{type=info}
Sentiment is a context indicator, not a signal generator. It answers "how crowded and one-sided
is the mood right now?" — never "should I enter here?". Treat the number as a description of the
weather, not an instruction.
:::

## Greed and euphoria: a zone for caution

**What it is.** When the index sits in extreme greed, the crowd is euphoric — leveraged and already
long. Euphoria is the emotional state where people buy *because* price is rising and cannot imagine
it falling.

**Why it warns you.** The contrarian logic is simple. If almost everyone who wanted to buy has
already bought, there are few buyers left to push price higher. And there are a lot of nervous late
longs who will sell fast the moment it dips. A market can only rise while there is fresh money still
willing to lift the offer; extreme greed is the condition where that fresh money is running out. So
extreme greed does not mean "sell now" — it means the ground is crowded: size down and stop chasing.

**In practice.** Concretely, this is what a greedy top *looks* like from the inside. The index has
sat above 90 for a week and funding is deeply positive (longs are paying shorts every few hours just
to hold the position). Your feed is wall-to-wall price targets, and every dip is bought within
minutes. Every one of those is a symptom of the *same* fact — the buy side is already all-in. That
is the moment to be smaller and more careful, not braver.

## Fear and capitulation: a zone of opportunity

**What it is.** At the other end, extreme fear means the crowd is scared. Its endpoint is
capitulation — the moment holders finally give up and sell at a loss just to stop the pain of
watching it fall further.

**Why it marks opportunity.** By the same logic inverted. Once the people who were going to panic
have already sold, the selling pressure that was driving price down is largely spent. Price is often
near a low. A market can only keep falling while there are still frightened holders left to sell.
Capitulation is the point where that supply exhausts itself.

**In practice.** Capitulation usually looks like a single violent session. BTC drops 15% in a few
hours on enormous volume, and a cascade of liquidations (forced closes of over-leveraged longs)
dumps even more supply into a thin book. The index gaps down to 6, and social flips to "it's over,
crypto is dead." That flush — forced selling and a spike of fear all at once — is what a
capitulation low can look like. This is a zone of interest, a place to *look* for setups, not a
command to buy.

## Why it is a zone, not a trigger

Some traders see the index hit 10 and buy, or hit 90 and sell, as if the number were an entry
button. **Extremes can persist for weeks or months.** Greed stayed extreme through entire bull runs
while price kept climbing; fear stayed extreme through long grinding bear markets while price kept
bleeding. A contrarian who shorted the *first* "extreme greed" print, or bought the *first* "extreme
fear" print, got run over. The crowd stayed lopsided far longer than their account could survive.

A sentiment extreme is a region where the odds and the risk/reward start to shift, and where you
should get *more careful* about the crowded side. It does not mark a moment. The trigger to enter
still has to come from your own tools: structure, a level, confirmation. Sentiment tells you *which
side is dangerous*; it never tells you *when*.

## Extremes are not timing

The classic trap is treating an extreme reading as a precise buy or sell signal. "Index at 8, back
up the truck" and "index at 92, short it" are how contrarians get liquidated early. They are
standing in front of a trend that is still being fed, on the strength of a number that only says the
crowd is lopsided. It does not say the lopsidedness is about to end. An extreme can deepen and stay
extreme for a long time, and a fixed-size contrarian bet placed *at* the extreme has to survive all
of it.

There is a second, quieter trap: double-counting. Half the index is price in disguise. A beginner
will "confirm" a bullish price breakout by pointing at rising greed — and think they have two
independent reasons when they really have one reason counted twice.

:::note{type=warning}
**How this burns people.** Extremes tell you the crowd is one-sided; they say nothing about
timing, and they do not turn the moment you notice them. Never treat 8 or 92 as an entry button, and
never mistake a greedy reading for independent confirmation of a rising price — it is largely the same
price, relabelled. Wait for a trigger from your own structure and levels, and size for the fact that
the extreme can get more extreme.
:::

## The mood never gets a night off

Crypto sharpens all of this. Traditional markets close; crypto runs 24/7, so the mood never gets a
nightly reset. Sentiment compounds through overnight and weekend sessions on thin liquidity. An
extreme can be reached *and* unwound while you sleep. The index you check at breakfast may already
have swung from panic to relief and be lagging both moves.

Crypto euphoria is also socially driven to a degree stocks rarely see. A single viral thread, an
influencer call, or a new exchange listing can spike social volume, search interest, and price *at
the same time*. Three of the index's inputs feed on those things, so one narrative can push the
whole gauge into extreme greed fast. Persistent positive funding can then bankroll that greed. As
long as longs are willing to keep paying to hold, the crowded, euphoric state can last far longer
than "it must be exhausted" suggests.

Finally, the headline index tracks Bitcoin and the broad market, not each coin. A single alt can
have a wildly euphoric or terrified crowd of its own while the market-wide index reads calm. It
happens on a thin book, where a handful of orders moves price and sentiment violently. In alts,
sentiment is even more local and faster than the one number on the dashboard suggests.
