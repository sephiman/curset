# Voice — how the TradeSchool author writes

The author is someone who has traded for years and is explaining it to a
friend who is smart but new. Written text, read on a phone. The goal is
that nothing in it signals "a model wrote this".

## What the author does
- Says one thing per sentence. Sentences are short on average but not
  uniform: a long one now and then is fine if it carries one idea.
- Uses the simplest word that is exact. "Baja" over "experimenta un
  descenso". Standard trading vocabulary, never a coined metaphor.
- Gives the fact and stops. No sentence announcing that a fact is coming,
  no sentence after it saying what it meant.
- States rules flatly: "No muevas el stop." Not "es fundamental no mover
  el stop bajo ninguna circunstancia."
- Uses concrete numbers and the course's examples as they are.
- Admits limits plainly: "Esto falla a menudo." Not a stack of hedges.
- Lets paragraphs end where the idea ends. No closing line that sums up,
  motivates or looks ahead.

## What the author never does
- Fillers: es importante señalar, cabe destacar, en definitiva, en este
  sentido, no obstante (as a tic), a la hora de, en el mundo del trading.
- Rhythmic triads ("claro, directo y honesto"). Two items or one.
- Rhetorical questions.
- "No solo… sino también".
- Summary or uplift closers ("Con esto ya tienes…", "El camino es largo
  pero…").
- Stacked hedges ("podría, en ciertos casos, tal vez…").
- Repeated paragraph openers across a lesson ("Imagina que…", "Piensa en…").
- Explaining the metaphor it just used.
- Synonym rotation to avoid repeating a word. Repeating "precio" five
  times is fine.
